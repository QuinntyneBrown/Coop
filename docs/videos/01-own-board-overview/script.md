# 01 · A website and a helping hand for OWN

## Welcome

Hello, and thank you for having us.

Some of us love the internet. Some of us would rather keep things simple, and that is perfectly fine. This platform is for everyone. It is a website, and it is also a helping hand.

Today we will show you six things. Each one is something you told us matters. For each one, we will say plainly what works today, and what comes next.

[pause 2s]

## Six things that matter

First, a website that is beautiful and easy to read. This is the home page we built for OWN. It is warm. It has big words, big buttons, and pictures of home. It works today, on a computer and on a phone.

[pause 2s]

Second, OWN runs it. Nobody else. Someone in your office signs in to the admin site. It is the same site members sign in to, but the office also sees the office areas. Every purple tag on this screen marks a part of the website that the office can change by themselves. The words, the photos, the menu.

All of it lives in one place, called Website. It lists everything a visitor sees, laid out the way they see it. The home page, the pages, the menu and footer, the board of directors, the photos and the look.

On the home page, the office can change the words of each section, move a section up or down, or hide it for a while. A hidden section is marked Hidden, and its words and photos are kept.

The office can also add a whole page. Each page starts with a banner, a title and a photo, and then a heading, a paragraph, a list or a photo, one after another. A tick box, show this page in the website menu, lets the office write a page before anyone can see it.

Changes go into a draft first. Preview site shows the draft, with a banner that says it is a preview. When it looks right, the office presses Publish, and visitors see it. Or Discard changes, to start again.

And when you hold your yearly election, the office updates the board of directors in one list, and the new directors appear on the website the same afternoon. No programmer needed.

[pause 2s]

Third, notices that nobody misses. The office writes a notice once. It shows on the front page of the website, and in the documents page for members. This works today.

On her profile, a member can choose to get new notices by email, by text message, or both. When the office publishes a notice, one tick sends it to everyone who asked. This works today too. The one step left is connecting a real email and text message service, so the messages actually go out.

[pause 2s]

Fourth, a leaking tap should be easy to report. A member taps new request, types a few words, adds a photo if she likes, and sends it.

The office sees it at once, and taps receive, so the member knows someone is on it. When the work begins, the office taps start work, and when it is fixed, finish work. The member can follow the request from new, to received, to started, to completed, with her photos beside it. All of this works today.

[pause 2s]

Fifth, bigger words. Any member can make the text bigger, all the way to four hundred percent, and still use the whole site. Everything stacks into one easy column, and nothing falls off the edge. This works on every screen today.

[pause 2s]

Sixth, your own face. A member can add her own profile picture, change it, or take it away. The office sees her face beside the requests she makes. This works today.

[pause 2s]

## Today and next

So, in short. All six things work today. The home page and the pages the office edits and publishes, the board list, the colours and the photo library, notices by email or text, repair requests with photos, bigger text, and profile pictures. Coming next is connecting a real email and text message service, so those notices actually go out.

[pause 2s]
