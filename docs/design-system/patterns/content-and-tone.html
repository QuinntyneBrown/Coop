Content and tone

The admin speaks to co-op staff and board members the way a capable, kind colleague would: plainly, calmly and without hurry. Much of what they write is read by the women who live in the co-op, so the admin helps them write plainly too.

Overview

The base rules (sentence case, the error formula, the empty-state formula, numbers and dates) live in foundations: content. This page covers what is particular to the Coop admin: who is reading, the one voice it uses, how that voice changes for text that members will read, and the strings the admin already uses, so new copy matches them.

The people at the keyboard are staff and volunteer board members of OWN Housing Co-op. Many are older themselves, some use the admin only now and then, and none should need training to understand a screen. The people they serve are retired, older women who read the notices, pages and website the admin publishes.

When to use

The voice follows the reader of the words, not the screen they are typed on.

  • Every admin screen, dialog and message (Dashboard, Maintenance, Documents, Pages, Board, Website, Messages, Users, Roles & Privileges, Profiles, Assets, Invitations, Settings, sign-in and onboarding) uses the admin voice.
  • Text staff write for members (notice titles and content, page text, the website's blocks, director roles) uses the member voice. The admin cannot write it for them, but its help text nudges them: "Add a page, write it in plain words, and it is live straight away."
  • Help text that explains who will see something is written for the admin user about members: "Only members who asked for new notices on their profile get one."

Responsive behaviour

  • Write for a 320px-wide screen first, which is what a laptop shows at 400% zoom. A button label that wraps there is too long; shorten the verb phrase.
  • Names, titles and usernames are never shortened in copy. They wrap in headings and lists; they truncate only in a dense table cell, with the full text available on the row's own screen.
  • Paragraphs stop at about 65 characters wide (--layout-measure); empty-state text at 36.
  • Page intros stay to one or two sentences so the screen's content is visible without scrolling on a laptop.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Plain language is the pattern. Aim for words a person reads once and understands, short sentences, and no abbreviation without its full form. Every page has lang="en".

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabEvery link and button label makes sense read alone in a list of controls: "Edit mira.fern", "Delete Autumn courtyard gathering", not "Edit" or "Click here".

Focus

Headings and first sentences carry the point, so someone landing on a screen's <h1> hears what matters first. Instructions never say "click"; say "select" or name the button.

Labelling

Every field has a visible label in sentence case ("First name", "Invitation code"); placeholders are hints, never the label. Required fields say so in the label. Never refer to something by its colour or position alone ("the green button", "on the right").

Respect

  • Members are "members" or, on the public site, "residents" or "neighbours". Use their names. Never describe them by age ("the elderly", "seniors", "old ladies") or with endearments ("dear").
  • Never imply a member is confused or forgetful. Explain the process, not the person: "Members who asked for notices get one", not "Members who can’t check the site".
  • Staff and board are colleagues, not operators. Thank them only for real effort, never for routine saves.

Case and punctuation

  • Sentence case everywhere: page titles, buttons, tabs, column headers, field labels and badges ("Add user", "Maintenance requests", "First name"). Badges are set in capitals by style, not by typing them.
  • Curly quotes and apostrophes: ’ “ ”. Quote the names of things in messages: “About us”.
  • Ellipsis (…) only on progress words: "Loading the board list…". Not on placeholders: "Part of a username", not "Search users...".
  • No full stop on titles, labels, buttons or badges; full stops on every sentence, including one-sentence messages.
  • No exclamation marks in the admin. A welcome is warm without one: "Welcome to OWN Housing Co-op".

Drift from the implemented admin

The admin speaks with two voices. The newer, hand-written screens (Board, Pages, Website and notices) already use the voice on this page. The older generated screens differ:

  • Title Case in headings, buttons, labels and columns: "Maintenance Requests", "Add User", "New Request", "Create Invitation", "Sign In", "First Name", "Confirm Delete". The system uses sentence case.
  • System words: "Invalid username or password", "Failed to update password", "Registration failed. Please check your invitation token.", "Submit", "Invitation Token". The system says what happened and what to do, and calls the token an invitation code.
  • Rule-style field errors: "Username is required", "Please confirm your password". The system writes "Enter your username.", "Type the same password again."
  • Confirmations: "Are you sure you want to delete mira.fern?" with a bare "Delete". The system asks "Delete mira.fern?" and names the action, "Delete user".
  • "Successfully": "Theme saved successfully", "Profile updated successfully", "Password updated successfully". See notifications.
  • Stored values on screen: the Users table shows SystemAdministrator and BoardMember; the Add user dialog offers "Member, Board Member, Admin"; Invitations offer "Member, Board Member, Staff". The system uses one set of role words everywhere.
  • Abbreviations and exclamations: the maintenance category "HVAC"; "Welcome to Your Cooperative!" and "You’re all set! Click below to go to your dashboard." in onboarding. The system spells terms out, drops the exclamation marks and never says "click".
  • Placeholders as labels with three dots: "Search users...", "Search documents...". See lists and tables.
