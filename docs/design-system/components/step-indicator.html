Step indicator

Where someone is in a short set-up flow: a row of dots, one of them stretched, and the words "Step 2 of 3". The words carry the information; the dots are decoration.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stablep.steps

Overview

Use a step indicator at the top of every step of a short, linear flow so the person knows how much is left. The admin has one such flow: onboarding, three steps long, which a new user sees once after signing up. Step 1 welcomes them, step 2 sets up their profile and step 3 asks which unit they live in.

  • Not navigation. The dots are not links or buttons. Back and Next on the step move between steps. For pages inside a section use the sidebar, and see breadcrumb.
  • Not progress. It counts steps, not work done. For an upload in progress use progress.
  • See the wizard step pattern for the whole step: indicator, heading, fields and actions.

Anatomy

  1. Done dot.steps__dot--done: the accent at half opacity, one for each finished step.
  2. Current dot.steps__dot--current: full accent, stretched to --space-5 wide, so the eye finds it without reading.
  3. Dot to come.steps__dot: --color-border-strong, --space-2 round.
  4. Label"Step 2 of 3" in --text-caption, muted. This is the text people and screen readers read; the dots are aria-hidden.

Variants

One variant. The number of dots follows the number of steps; admin onboarding has three.

Sizes

The indicator has one size at every width.

States

The indicator is static: no hover, focus, active or disabled, because nothing in it can be pressed. Each dot is in one of three states.

Step 2 of 3

  • Answer given on the step: choosing a unit on step 3 (the unit-selected state) does not change the indicator. It counts steps, not answers.
  • Skipped step: when someone presses Skip on step 2, step 2 still shows as done on step 3. The dots say "you have been past this", not "this is complete".

Responsive behaviour

  • One row at every width. Three dots and the label need under 10rem, so they fit at 320px CSS width and at 400% zoom.
  • Centred at the top of the onboarding card at every width; the card's .stack puts --space-4 between it and the heading.
  • The label never shortens to "2/3".

Theming

Dots to come use --color-border-strong; the current and done dots use --color-accent; the label uses --color-fg-muted. All are semantic tokens, so the dark theme comes free. The done dots' half opacity is applied to the dot, not a second colour token.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A paragraph of text, not a widget. The dots are aria-hidden; the words "Step 2 of 3" carry the meaning and are read as ordinary text at the top of the step. A list with aria-current="step" would add nothing here, because the steps have no names of their own to list.

Keyboard

Nothing is focusable, so there is no keyboard map. The first tab stop on the step is its first field or button.

Focus

No focus ring of its own. When Next or Back loads a new step, move focus to the step's <h1> (with tabindex="-1") so a screen reader starts at the top; the indicator just above it has already said "Step 2 of 3" when read in order.

Labelling

The step's heading follows straight after, so a screen reader hears "Step 2 of 3. Set up your profile". The label is never the only place the step is named, and the step is never named only by the dots.

The current dot is told apart by its width as well as its colour, so it does not rely on colour alone (WCAG 1.4.1). The half-opacity done dots are decorative; the label carries the same information.

Motion

When the step changes, the current dot stretches over --duration-base. Under reduced motion the tokens zero the duration and it simply changes.

Touch

Not a target. Nothing in it responds to a tap, so people cannot press a dot by mistake.

Content

  • The label is always "Step N of M", with the step the person is on, never the step just finished.
  • The welcome counts as step 1, so the dots start filling from the first screen and the length of the flow is known before it starts.
  • Step headings follow in sentence case: "Welcome to your cooperative", "Set up your profile", "Select your unit".

Drift from the implemented admin

  • Markup. The admin uses div.step-indicator with empty div.step-dot children and puts "Step 1 of 3" in a separate unstyled div. The system groups both in p.steps and hides the dots with aria-hidden.
  • Current dot. Done and current dots both get .active (a green fill, 10px round); .current only adds a 3px ring of the same green at 20% opacity, far below 3:1. The system stretches the current dot to --space-5 and pales the done dots, in the violet accent.
  • Headings. Each step's title is an <h2> and the page has no <h1>; titles are in Title Case ("Welcome to Your Cooperative!", "Set Up Your Profile", "Select Your Unit"). The system makes the step title the page's <h1> in sentence case.
  • Landmark. The onboarding page has no <main>; the mock capture adds role="main" to the app root. The system puts the card in main.auth-layout (see layout primitives).
  • Global. The admin's font is Outfit and its theme is light only; the system uses rounded system fonts and both themes.
