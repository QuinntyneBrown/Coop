Divider

A thin line that separates two groups of content: a plain rule between parts of a card or dialog, or a line with a short word in the middle ("or") between two ways of doing the same thing.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stableNative <hr> or <div>

Overview

Use a divider when two groups inside one surface need a visible break and nothing else draws one. Most separation in the admin comes for free and needs no divider:

  • Rows of a list (recent requests, recent notices) are separated by .list__item's own border.
  • Rows of a table, the header and footer of a dialog, the sidebar's footer and the phone tab bar draw their own edges.
  • Separate cards and page sections are spaced apart with .stack and .section (see layout primitives), not lined.

That leaves two uses:

  • Rule (<hr class="divider divider--rule">): between parts of one card or dialog body, such as a maintenance request's description and its photos.
  • Labelled (<div class="divider"><span>or</span></div>): between two alternatives, such as choosing a photo from Assets or uploading a new one in the photo picker.

Anatomy

  1. LineA hairline (--border-width-hairline) in --color-border-default. On the labelled divider, two lines fill the space either side of the label.
  2. Label (labelled divider only)One or two words in --text-body-sm, --color-fg-muted, with --space-3 either side. It is read aloud as ordinary text.

Sizes

One weight (a hairline) and one label size (--text-body-sm).

States

A divider cannot be pressed or focused, so it has no hover, focus, active or disabled state. Both themes are shown under Variants.

Responsive behaviour

  • Both variants fill the width of their container at every size.
  • The labelled divider's lines shrink around the label; a one-word label fits at 320px CSS width and at 400% zoom. A longer label would wrap onto two lines centred between the lines, which is one reason to keep it to one word.
  • Spacing does not change with the screen: the rule keeps --space-4 above and below.

Theming

The line reads --color-border-default and the label --color-fg-muted. Both have light and dark values, so the divider sits on a card, a dialog or the page in either theme without changes. To make a line stronger in one place, do not add a colour: ask whether the two groups belong in separate cards.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

The rule is a native <hr>, which has the implicit separator role (see the ARIA separator role); screen readers announce it as a break between the groups. The labelled divider is a <div>: its word is read as text ("or") and its lines are CSS generated content, so it is not given a separator role.

Keyboard

Not focusable, so there is no keyboard map; Tab passes over it to the next control.

Focus

None of its own.

Labelling

A rule needs no label; the headings or labels of the groups on either side say what they are. Never use a labelled divider in place of a heading: "Photos" above the photos is a heading or a label, not a divider word.

Contrast

Label on card
Label in a dialog

The line itself is decorative: the groups' own headings and spacing carry the structure, so WCAG 1.4.11 does not ask it to reach 3:1.

Motion

None.

Touch

Not a target. Keep at least --space-4 between the controls on either side so that a divider never squeezes two targets together.

Content

  • A labelled divider's word is lower case and short: "or". It joins two choices; it does not name a section.
  • A rule has no text.

ClassEffect
.dividerOn a <div>: a centred, muted label with a hairline filling each side.
.divider--ruleWith .divider on an <hr>: a single hairline with --space-4 above and below; the side lines are removed.

Put .divider on a <div>, not a <p>: the base style caps every paragraph at --layout-measure, so a paragraph divider stops short of the card's edge in a wide card.

Sources

No admin mock uses an <hr> or a divider class. These mocks draw their breaks with borders on feature classes:

  • dialogs/admin-photo-picker/default (the upload section starts with a top border under the asset library)
  • pages/admin-maintenance/detail-new (a request's details, then its photos and actions)
  • pages/admin-dashboard/default (card headers and rows of recent requests and notices)
  • dialogs/admin-document-viewer/default (bordered header and footer)
  • pages/admin-messaging/chat (bordered chat header and message bar)

Drift from the implemented admin

  • No divider component. Breaks are borders declared feature by feature: .card-header, .request-item and .notice-item on the dashboard; .viewer-header and .viewer-footer in the document viewer; .chat-header and .message-input-bar in Messages; .sidebar-header and .sidebar-footer; .detail-tabs on Maintenance; .photo-upload in the photo picker. The system uses .divider--rule inside a body and lets lists, tables, dialogs and the sidebar draw their own edges with --color-border-default.
  • Two border colours. Most borders are a light warm grey; the dashboard's request and notice rows use a near-white grey that is barely visible on a white card. The system has one border token per role.
  • No labelled divider. The photo picker separates "choose from the library" from "Upload a new photo" with a plain top border. The system offers div.divider with "or" for that choice.
  • Light only. The admin's border greys are fixed values; the system's tokens change with the theme.
