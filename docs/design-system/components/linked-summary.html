Linked summary

What the visitor will see in a section whose items are kept on another screen, with a count, the first items and one Manage link.

Overview

Two home page sections show content kept elsewhere: "What's on" lists the published notices (Community, Documents) and "Board of directors" lists the directors (Website, Board of directors). Their forms hold the heading fields, and under them the linked summary says what the visitor will see, with a count and the first items, and one link to the screen where it is changed: Manage notices or Manage directors.

  • Use it in a section form whose items come from another screen.
  • Do not use it to edit those items; the link leads to their own screen.

Anatomy

  1. Title"What visitors will see:" and the count.
  2. ItemsThe first few items as the public page orders them, or an empty sentence.
  3. Manage linkA secondary button-link with a trailing arrow to the other screen.

Variants

With items and empty. Empty says what shows instead and where to add items.

Responsive behaviour

  • Text and link sit side by side when there is room and stack at 320px, the link full width under the list.

Theming

A dashed --color-border-strong frame on the surface, so it reads as a window onto another screen rather than a field.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Static text and a link. The link is an <a> styled as a secondary button because it navigates.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabReaches the Manage link after the fields above it.
EnterOpens the other screen in the same tab.

Focus

The shared ring on the link. Coming back with the browser's Back returns to the editor with the same section selected.

Labelling

The link names its destination: "Manage notices", "Manage directors". The arrow icon is decorative.

Content

  • Title: "What visitors will see:" then a count with its noun and order: "3 published notices, newest first", "4 directors, in this order", "no published notices".
  • Items: title and date for notices; name and role for directors; at most five, then nothing more (no "and 3 more").
  • Empty: what shows instead and where to add items, in two sentences.
